  1. 19AThe ear was deaf on arrival, the fix works, and the board answered

    TinyOS answered a human. Three commands typed at ti64dink --console, three distinct sequence-matched refusals spoken back over the cable, nothing unanswered — M1's round trip proven on silicon, and STORY-P1-09-17 criterion 4's refusal arms closed. Getting there took three findings. LE-119 closed in code and on silicon, putting the RX and CMD rows on the wire as rotation slots (cadence unmoved) — and its very first capture carried LE-118's deciding evidence: a freshly netbooted board stopped reason=nobuffer accepted=0 refused=0 with nothing sent to it, unmoved by three pings. The ear was deaf before our traffic, not because of it. The owner ruled BNA becomes a counted drop, OVR stays terminal — two different failures wearing one word, since BNA is backpressure about a frame that never entered the ring — and the next boot read state=listening accepted=5 refused=19 dropped=19: alive, classifying, with the contention finally a number. LE-122 is the last gap and it is measured, not guessed: every command refused oversize, so the board was made to state its own width; the first reading (47) was the copy-out cap saturating, so the buffer was widened and the board answered lastlen=50 against 46 sent — exactly the four octets of the FCS. Also: LE-120's general half (the transport refuses and speaks TOS64-TRUNC/1; capacity derived from the transcript, after the test found seven-digit latency tails would overflow the old bound), and main's inherited red cleared — a test literal pinning qualified_count()==0 had been calling the project's first qualified platform a violation.

  2. 20AThe board answered the question it was asked, and the ear was measured one arm at a time

    M2. PING and STATUS were answered, not refused, and STATUS carried a fact only the board holds — its own boot verdict, read out of the running kernel because a human asked. The unknown verb is now refused by the verb table (unknown-verb) rather than by the size check, which is what makes the answers evidence that deny-by-default was consulted at all. It took exactly the one register bit and the one boot LE-122 predicted: MACB_DRFCS_OFFSET 17 transcribed from macb.h and set at the MAC — never subtracted in the glue, because a later FCS strip would silently invert a software -4 and every command would refuse as undersize instead. TOS64-CMD/1 now reads lastlen=46 where it read 50. STORY-P1-09-16 criterion 4 then closed in full: five arms, ten frames each, from a settled counter — ping and unicast moved accepted by exactly ten, ethertype and prefix moved refused by exactly ten, and notforus moved nothing, including dropped: the frame never entered the ring, which is the only arm that tests the containment argument rather than the classifier. LE-117's tripwire fired for the first time and the record survives it — the bootloader EEPROM reads byte-identical to the pin — but the reading also found rpi-eeprom-update enabled with nothing held, so the row's stated trigger is live and its half (2) is now an owner decision on a measured fact. LE-121's refusal is built (clock_unstable, firing before any distribution exists) and the row stays open on one silicon run: the discriminator had to become a second mode rather than a spread, because the derivable spread bound would have refused every excursion past ~24 ticks — a guard that swallows the one thing Q3 exists to report.   3. 21AThe destination, and why a week of good work did not approach it

    A mandate, not a record — no code, no row closed, no evidence filed. Written at the owner's finding that a week of development ends “staring at an OS with a splash screen.” The diagnosis is structural and visible in one manifest line: shell and exec are not in the board image at all. The human-usable OS — TINYCMD's verb core, the policy seam, the labelled volume, the DOS front-end and its byte-exact parity gate — exists and is tested on x86_64 under QEMU, while the Pi 5 image carries only instruments. So the board can be proven and cannot be used, and every board session this week could only ever add evidence. The chain is three steps, not a phase: get shell compiling for the board; carry it in the image and widen 20A's proven command channel onto its verb core with the containment argument re-made; point ti64dink --console at it — which is a human at a TinyOS prompt over Ethernet, the owner's standing priority (1), from parts that already exist. Step 1's obstacle was measured rather than assumed: cargo check -p shell --lib for AArch64 fails with every error inside hal-x86_64 and none in shell's own source — an unconditional path dependency that exists for the crate's QEMU fixture binary. Also names the process change: the destination selects the work and the defect register stops setting the agenda; loose ends get triaged as blocking-a-step or not, staying open on purpose; and the hand-syncing that ate 20A gets automated, since emit-dashboard and emit-feasibility already print what was edited by hand.
