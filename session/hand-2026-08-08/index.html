  1. 19AThe ear was deaf on arrival, the fix works, and the board answered

    TinyOS answered a human. Three commands typed at ti64dink --console, three distinct sequence-matched refusals spoken back over the cable, nothing unanswered — M1's round trip proven on silicon, and STORY-P1-09-17 criterion 4's refusal arms closed. Getting there took three findings. LE-119 closed in code and on silicon, putting the RX and CMD rows on the wire as rotation slots (cadence unmoved) — and its very first capture carried LE-118's deciding evidence: a freshly netbooted board stopped reason=nobuffer accepted=0 refused=0 with nothing sent to it, unmoved by three pings. The ear was deaf before our traffic, not because of it. The owner ruled BNA becomes a counted drop, OVR stays terminal — two different failures wearing one word, since BNA is backpressure about a frame that never entered the ring — and the next boot read state=listening accepted=5 refused=19 dropped=19: alive, classifying, with the contention finally a number. LE-122 is the last gap and it is measured, not guessed: every command refused oversize, so the board was made to state its own width; the first reading (47) was the copy-out cap saturating, so the buffer was widened and the board answered lastlen=50 against 46 sent — exactly the four octets of the FCS. Also: LE-120's general half (the transport refuses and speaks TOS64-TRUNC/1; capacity derived from the transcript, after the test found seven-digit latency tails would overflow the old bound), and main's inherited red cleared — a test literal pinning qualified_count()==0 had been calling the project's first qualified platform a violation.

  2. 20AThe board answered the question it was asked, and the ear was measured one arm at a time

  3. 21AThe destination, and why a week of good work did not approach it

    A mandate, not a record — no code, no row closed, no evidence filed. Written at the owner's finding that a week of development ends “staring at an OS with a splash screen.” The diagnosis is structural and visible in one manifest line: shell and exec are not in the board image at all. The human-usable OS — TINYCMD's verb core, the policy seam, the labelled volume, the DOS front-end and its byte-exact parity gate — exists and is tested on x86_64 under QEMU, while the Pi 5 image carries only instruments. So the board can be proven and cannot be used, and every board session this week could only ever add evidence. The chain is three steps, not a phase: get shell compiling for the board; carry it in the image and widen 20A's proven command channel onto its verb core with the containment argument re-made; point ti64dink --console at it — which is a human at a TinyOS prompt over Ethernet, the owner's standing priority (1), from parts that already exist. Step 1's obstacle was measured rather than assumed: cargo check -p shell --lib for AArch64 fails with every error inside hal-x86_64 and none in shell's own source — an unconditional path dependency that exists for the crate's QEMU fixture binary. Also names the process change: the destination selects the work and the defect register stops setting the agenda; loose ends get triaged as blocking-a-step or not, staying open on purpose; and the hand-syncing that ate 20A gets automated, since emit-dashboard and emit-feasibility already print what was edited by hand.

  4. 22AThe usable OS is on the board

    The first session to open 21A rather than the register to choose its work, and it advanced §3 steps 1 and 2. TINYCMD now compiles for AArch64, links into every Pi 5 image variant, and answers a command frame with its own output. Step 1 was one manifest line, measured before it was touched: 29 errors, every one inside hal-x86_64, none in shell's own source — an unconditional dependency that existed for an x86_64 fixture binary, now cfg(target_arch = "x86_64"). kernel was left alone and that was measured too: only one of 21A's two dependencies ever blocked, and gating the other would break the host suite on an AArch64 machine. The gate went in first — shell --lib is now a board-target entry in check-boot-images (LE-123, raised and closed). Step 2 gave TOS64-CMD/1 a third row, SHELL, and -17's expiring absence argument was re-made rather than relaxed: the runner rebuilds its World from a const seed on every command, so the board after any admitted sequence is bit-identical to the board before it; the grant set is the read-only subset of the verb core over a volume the image seeded; every mutating verb and every live-kernel-state verb (MEM, TASKMGR, SPOOR) is withheld as a written decision, waiting on the same session/authentication story. The run happens inside the bounded answer slot, so a command costs the beat its answer always cost — one frame in, one line out, with what did not fit counted in a more= field. Real output, from the shipped composition: DIR returns a full directory listing on one line of wire; DEL returns Access denied … [audited] naming the WIRE session. Flattened image 323,400 → 525,624 octets, 6.3% of the 8 MB ceiling, measured both sides. Ti64Dink's two-directions parity test went red on the third row and now sends SHELL <command> and un-escapes the answer. One defect found that no test was looking for (LE-124): VER answers x86_64 on an AArch64 board, and the string is pinned byte-exact by the golden-transcript gate that exists to catch exactly that — left open on purpose, because regenerating a golden in the session that widened a hostile-input surface is two reviews wearing one hat. And then the cable. BOOT_ORDER=0xf12 is network-then-SD, so serving the image and cycling the plug is the whole deployment: tos64-netboot confirmed 1027 blocks with the digest matched at both ends, tos64-power cycled and confirmed by readback, and the board came up fixture=measure ok=true. A person then typed FIND /N "cable" README.TXT and a Raspberry Pi 5 answered with TINYCMD's own numbered match out of its own RAM volume — nine exchanges, 0 exchange(s) went unanswered, DEL and SET each refused by name and audited against the WIRE session, WOBBLE refused by the verb table, and a DIR byte-identical before and after two denied mutations. The board's own row corroborates independently: TOS64-CMD/1 last=SHELL answered=17 refused=2 lastlen=144 — and lastlen=144 is exactly COMMAND_PAYLOAD_BYTES, so LE-122's FCS strip still holds at the widened envelope. Mid-session the envelope went 46 → 144 octets, correcting a quantifier rather than changing a mind: -17 made the frame exactly the Ethernet minimum, but a NIC pads up to the minimum and never pads a frame already at it, so the property needed is >= not == — and pinning the floor had cost the command line 98 octets. ARGUMENT_BYTES is now 128, derived: exactly shell::capacities::MAX_LINE, held by a const assertion, so the wire carries every line the shell can read. Three timeouts on a first run turned out to be the board answering late during its boot campaign (answered=17 accounts for them) — and the console correctly called them TIMEOUT and not refusal, its rule 1 working under the first real condition that ever tested it. Owner instruction taken up the same session: every handshake gets a gate, and the gate gets a mutation. Two unguarded handshakes on this path — the escape pair and the answer's field names — now read the Rust source in both directions, and each was mutated until it failed before being trusted.   5. 23AThe keyhole, the clamp, and the lie

    Three fixes taken at the owner's direction after a review of 22A's own SWOT — two it identified and recorded rather than fixed, one it correctly deferred. The command line went from 30 octets to 128, and the keyhole turns out to have been self-imposed: COMMAND_PAYLOAD_BYTES was pinned so the frame was exactly the Ethernet minimum, but a NIC pads a short frame up to 60 and never pads one already at or above it — padding immunity always needed >= and was written with ==. The new width is derived: 128 is shell::capacities::MAX_LINE, the widest line the board's own verb core accepts, and because hal-arm64 cannot name shell without a cycle, the composition root holds the two constants equal at compile time. Cost, measured: 104 octets of image for 98 octets of command line. The arch-only clamp got tests — hal-arm64/src/wire_shell.rs went from zero to five, its arithmetic extracted arch-neutral so a host test can reach it (LE-66's shape), and stage_line now zeroes the field tail so a short line cannot show the far side what the last command left behind. LE-124 closed: Platform { tier, arch } is injected into World exactly as tasks and spoors already are, the board supplies its own Tier 1 / aarch64 — the same pair TOS64-MEAS/2 announces — and the golden transcript did not change, so the regeneration the row budgeted for was avoidable. Every guard was mutation-verified: six deliberate breakages, six reds, and the width disagreement is a compile error rather than a test failure. Names the two constraints that now bind — a 256-octet answer line (a two-file DIR already truncates) and one answer per ~1 Hz park beat, which is SEC-20's containment rather than a knob.

  6. 24AThe GUI gets a board tab, and a regression no gate could see

    The operator console can now open a tab whose commands run on the Raspberry Pi rather than in-process on the laptop. TabKind::Board joins Dos and Parity: a DOS tab runs the verb core on this laptop, a board tab runs nothing locally — the line crosses the cable as one TOS64-CMD/1 frame and the board's own TINYCMD answers. They render identically because it is the same crate (what FEAT-P2-01's fmt::Write sink bought) but only one of them is TinyOS, so the chrome names it BOARD. The seam is a BoardLink trait with seven tests behind a scripted double, so the tab model is provable with no Pi on the desk; the width limit is named from shell::capacities::MAX_LINE rather than restated; a board tab cannot be opened without a link; and every failure is rendered rather than swallowed. Building it exposed LE-125: nothing in CI or any local gate compiles the console, so 23A's LE-124 fix broke the GUI and reached main past a fully green gate set — LE-72 and LE-92's third instance, on the artifact the owner named as the route to human usability. check-external-isolation gates the direction of the dependency and had been misread as evidence the PoC was healthy. Names what still binds: ~1 Hz answers and a 256-octet answer line.

  7. 25AA crate that gains its first test changes a gate, and says nothing

    24A's sibling rather than its sequel: that one found an artifact outside the workspace no gate compiles, this one found an artifact inside it no gate linted. CI run 31256380768 went red on a tree whose local gates were all green — two clippy::assertions_on_constants, fixed in 895bc82 as const { assert! }, which is the better form anyway since a board-stack bound and an unpaddable frame width should refuse to compile rather than fail a test run. The defect was one commit; why it reached the runner was not fixed by it. check-lints lints --lib --tests precisely so test code cannot escape it, and it would have caught both — but pi5-image was not in HOST_LINT_TARGETS, correctly, for the crate's whole life as a packaging stub with no tests and no library. STORY-P1-09-18 ended that silently by giving it its first #[cfg(test)] module. The durable form: a crate that gains its first test has just entered a lint gate's remit, and nothing in cargo announces it. Closed test-first as LE-126; the list is now enumerated and length-pinned, because a membership assertion catches a removal and only a length assertion catches the crate somebody adds without asking which gates own it. And the gate was mutated until it failed before being believed — where the first mutation attempt passed: it mutated size_of::<World>(), which reads like a constant assertion and is not one to clippy, so a session stopping at that green would have filed the gate as proven on the one arm that could never fail it. 20A §8's twin, and the sharper of the two: a gate must be mutated with the defect it exists for, not with something that resembles it. Also discharges half of 24A §4 item 1: cargo check -p stage-e-console is 47.6 s warm and compiles clean (independently confirming 24A's repair) — but warm is not CI, and the number does not argue against the seam-smoke option LE-125 already names.
