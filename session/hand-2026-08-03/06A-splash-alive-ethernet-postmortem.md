## Question 1 — why does the monitor show nothing?

"No signal" is a stronger observation than a dark picture: a granted, enabled
framebuffer scanning out our navy background would give the monitor sync. No
sync narrows the branches to these, none of which is yet eliminated:

1. **Observation conditions, still unconfirmed.** The framebuffer is
   negotiated once, at power-on, against the display the firmware sees then.
   Which micro-HDMI port (firmware default is HDMI0, beside USB-C), whether
   the monitor was connected and awake *before* power, and its input
   selection have not been established for any attempt, including this one.
   Until one attempt fixes those conditions deliberately, no stronger claim
   is honest.
2. **The mailbox framebuffer path fails silently on Pi 5 silicon.** This was
   always `STORY-P1-07-07`'s named blind-flight risk: the classic property
   interface (set-size / set-depth / allocate / get-pitch) is
   "undocumented-stable" on Pi 5, whose firmware display architecture
   changed materially from Pi 4 — it is a live possibility that the firmware
   answers the mailbox exchange but never connects the allocation to
   scan-out, or refuses the exchange entirely. Our splash fails **silently
   by design** (silent-continue into the park), so a refusing firmware and a
   perfect splash are indistinguishable on screen. Notably, the visual
   heartbeat only animates if the splash exchange succeeded — so "no signal"
   is consistent both with a refused exchange *and* with wrong plug
   conditions.
3. **A fault between verdict and splash** would also park dark — but it
   would be reported over the UART, which nobody can read yet (Question 2,
   cause 4).

**The discriminator is the serial line, again.** The board writes its
`TOS64-LINK/1` verdict and now heartbeats every second; a decoded heartbeat
proves the park loop is alive (eliminating branch 3), and a follow-up build
can add a `fb=granted|refused` field to the heartbeat to split branches 1
and 2 with no monitor at all. That field is the cheapest next diagnostic and
should be added before the next card swap.

## Question 2 — why was the Ethernet dead?

Layered causes, from the bottom up — each alone sufficient for a dead-flat
wire; the session peeled them in order:

1. **The PHY was never out of reset.** The BCM54213PE sits behind an
   **active-low reset on RP1 GPIO 32** (`bcm2712-rpi-5-b.dts`:
   `phy-reset-gpios = <&rp1_gpio 32 GPIO_ACTIVE_LOW>`, 5 ms hold). On Linux
   the PHY driver releases it; bare-metal, nothing did. A PHY in reset does
   not negotiate and is electrically indistinguishable from an absent
   device — precisely what the laptop NIC saw across every watch (`LE-68`).
   `STORY-P1-09-04` implements the release (glitch-ordered RIO sequence);
   **not yet verified on the board.**
2. **Beneath that, the PCIe link to RP1 was firmware-reset.** Absent
   `pciex4_reset=0` the firmware tears down the ×4 link before kernel entry,
   taking all of RP1 — GEM, GPIO, everything — out of reach. The keep-line
   is now generated by the build and pinned (`STORY-P1-09-01`); **whether
   this firmware version honors it is unproven** — 05A's four-branch
   uncertainty stands until a `TOS64-LINK/1` line is read.
3. **Above both, the driver chain never existed by design.** `LE-26` has
   said since 2026-07-28 that Pi 5 Ethernet means PCIe + RP1 + NIC/DMA
   drivers; `FEAT-P1-07` §6 deliberately excluded all of it. `FEAT-P1-09`
   walks a firmware-kept shortcut for *discovery only* — it was never going
   to move deploy bytes.
4. **And the instrument to see any of this is itself unverified**: four
   zero-byte serial captures on an adapter whose sixty-second loopback has
   never been run. The board has written its self-diagnosis on every boot
   since `0f8cf11b…`; nobody has been able to read one. The heartbeat build
   converts that from a timed conjunction (wiring ∧ clock ∧ operator
   timing) into an untimed listen — the twelve-baud sweep fires the moment
   the adapter re-enumerates on the laptop.

## Question 3 — why Ethernet cannot carry Ti64Dink (yet)

Ti64Dink — 04A's host application, renamed by owner order — is "the window
is the machine": the TinyOS UX rendered over an authenticated peer-to-peer
link. Ethernet cannot carry it today because **every layer between the
copper and the window is either unproven or deliberately not built**, and
the charter forbids skipping any of them:

1. **Physical:** link training has never been observed (`LE-68` unverified;
   `STORY-P1-09-02`/`-04` criterion 4 open). Until then there is no wire.
2. **Link layer:** `FEAT-P1-09` is transmit-only by contract — **receive is
   disabled and tested absent** (`RCG-01` at its strongest). A
   remote-desktop session needs RX, and RX on this kernel is a real driver:
   descriptor rings, bounded queues, hostile-frame validation, the C2
   device-service containment `LE-26` names — plus `LE-67`'s missing IOMMU
   story before a device may write RAM. None of it may be improvised past
   the assurance spine.
3. **No transport, no addresses:** no link-local addressing (IPv6-LL per
   `docs/deploy-protocol.md`), no IP, no datagram service — `EPIC-P3`
   territory, undecomposed.
4. **No authority layer:** the deploy-protocol/WCI session model
   (authenticated pairing, `deployer`/operator capability scopes, the ACI
   registry parked under `EPIC-P5`) is specification, not code. 04A's
   security line is absolute: the cable is *discovery*, never authority — a
   bare socket rendering frames would violate the charter on arrival. Even
   with a live wire tomorrow, Ti64Dink without the session layer is
   forbidden, not merely unfinished.
5. **No stream:** the UX frame source / input sink behind policy seams (04A
   milestone 4) exists on neither end; the Ti64 console's
   `ONE_WAY_TRANSPORT` refusal is the honest hole where the wire will land.

What Ethernet *can* already carry for Ti64Dink — the moment the wire is
proven — is exactly what `FEAT-P1-09` built: the **discovery signal**.
`TOS64-PRESENT/1` broadcasts are the plug-and-see trigger, and Ti64Dink's
first host-side Story (`FEAT-P2-10` under `EPIC-P2`) can be a link-watch +
beacon capture with zero device-side receive path. The order of work stands:
prove the wire → `LE-26` closure as a C2 device service → deploy-protocol
session → stream → chooser.
