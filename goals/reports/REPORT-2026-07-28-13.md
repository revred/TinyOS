## The two lines that carry the claim

**`effective_at_degrade=Some(25)` with `base_at_degrade=Some(2)`.** Both read from inside the ISR, on the tick `wcet::account_tick` applied the enforcement. The degrade landed on the task's own priority; the boost a blocked waiter depends on was untouched. That is `LE-22`'s boost-then-degrade half, with the timer driving it rather than a test harness.

**`effective_after_release=Some(2)`.** The holder left `unlock` on its declared floor, not the 5 it held before the overrun. That is the degrade-then-unlock half.

Between them, `medium at_block=0 at_degrade=0 at_release=0` with `ready=[true,true,true]`: an uninvolved task at priority 15, `Ready` at all three sampling points and demonstrably able to run afterwards (`final=1000`), made **no progress at all** across a window containing the degrade — and `dispatch order=[0, 2, 0, 2, 1]` shows it, medium's slot appearing only after the release.

## Clause 6 — the falsification, and what it revealed

Reverting the composition in `kernel::sched` (making `set_base_priority` also clear the inherited priority — the old single-field collision) turns the fixture red, three runs of three, with the defect named rather than a bare exit code:

```text
FAILED clause 3/7: medium was given CPU time while low still held the contended lock. This IS
the defect: with the boost discarded, low sits at its floor of 2, medium at 15 never yields,
and the holder can never reach its own unlock — the inversion G-RT-1 denies
FAILED clause 2: low's EFFECTIVE priority was still 25 — the blocked waiter kept its boost
across the degrade (this is LE-22's boost-then-degrade half)
...
enforcements=1 base_at_degrade=Some(2) effective_at_degrade=Some(2)
dispatch order=[0, 2, 0, 1]  stalled=true  medium_ticks_in_window=21
```

`effective_at_degrade` reads **2** where the fixed kernel reads 25, and the dispatch order shows medium taking the CPU immediately after the degrade instead of high.

**The falsification changed the fixture, which is the point of doing it.** On the first attempt the reverted kernel did not fail the fixture — it *hung*, and the harness killed it at the 15-second boot budget with an **empty serial capture**. That is not a quirk of the fixture: it is the defect's real consequence. With the boost discarded, low sits at 2, medium at 15 never yields, low can never reach its own `unlock`, so high stays `Blocked` forever and medium's own termination condition can never become true. A genuine deadlock — and exactly the `G-RT-1` unbounded-waiter-latency failure this subsystem exists to deny.

Detected but unexplained is the `LE-46` shape one level along: an instrument that fires and leaves nothing to read. The fixture now counts **ticks charged to medium while the holder still has the lock** — a quantity that is *exactly zero* in a passing run and grows every tick in a broken one — and ends the run when it exceeds 20, so the failure is reported instead of timed out. A wall-clock bound would have had to exceed a legitimate window of some hundreds of ticks, making it both slow to fire and sensitive to host speed; counting the inversion itself is neither.

## A fixture defect the stability loop caught, and the kernel did not have

Twelve consecutive runs were used rather than one. **Three failed**, reporting `released=false` alongside `restore_cost=Some(2)` and `effective_after_release=Some(2)` — the unlock had plainly happened, but the fixture's own record of it was missing.

The cause was in the fixture, not the kernel. `LOW_RELEASED = released` was assigned *after* the `without_interrupts` block. The unlock drops the holder to 2 and makes a task at 25 `Ready`, so from the instant interrupts come back the holder is preemptible between any two instructions — and once medium ends the run, it is never selected again, so the assignment is simply lost. The same latent window existed on the acquire path.

All of the evidence the checker reads is now captured **inside** the critical section that makes it true. Clause 4's "outranked, not retired" was also re-based off a phase-2 counter — whose value depends on whether the holder happens to get one more iteration before the next tick — onto two facts recorded at the release itself: the task's state, and whether medium outranks it. **20 of 20 runs pass** after the fix; the falsification still fails 3 of 3.

Worth stating plainly: a single green run would have shipped this. The intermittency was a fixture defect rather than a kernel one, but `LE-45` is an open row about an unexplained intermittent failure in this exact subsystem, and a fixture that loses its own evidence under preemption is the kind of thing that produces one.

## What is not claimed

- **Nothing about two contended locks (`LE-49`).** One lock, one waiter. `release_inheritance` still clears inheritance outright, and this fixture cannot see that because it never holds two.
- **Nothing about transitive inheritance.** Untouched.
- **No timing guardrail.** `D03`/`D05`/`D06` are selected because the composition governs dispatch and lock latency, not because anything here was measured. No `TINYOS-MEAS/2` envelope is emitted.
- **`LE-45` is still not explained.** This adds Tier 0 coverage next door to the fixture that logged the anomaly; it does not recover a diagnostic that was never captured. `LE-45` and `LE-46` stay open on their own terms — though the CI step added here runs under `--serial-capture` and retains the capture on failure, which is the `LE-46` remedy applied to the one fixture this Story owns rather than to the sweep.
- **Clippy was not run workspace-wide on the authoring host.** `hal-x86_64`'s `boot`/`interrupts`/`qemu_exit`/`serial` modules are `#[cfg(not(target_os = "windows"))]`, so `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets` cannot build the `no_std` binaries on Windows — a pre-existing condition affecting `exec`'s fixtures identically, not introduced here. Clippy was run against the real target instead (`-p kernel --target targets/x86_64-tinyos.json` with the fixture feature enabled) and is clean; CI runs the workspace form on Linux.
